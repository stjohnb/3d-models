The rungs need 5.9 m of dowel: three 2.4 m lengths cut as 3 x 600 + 460
twice and 3 x 460 once, which leaves room for saw kerf. The monkey-bar rungs
sit at 300 mm centres, so the 265 mm clear gaps between them stay outside the
89–230 mm rigid-opening head-entrapment band that a 250 mm pitch (215 mm
gaps) falls inside.

The wall boards run the full 2.0 m across the two slide-end corner posts,
from 100 mm above the ground to flush with the deck top, with 6 mm gaps. The
mid stile stands behind them at the centre, fixed to the deck rim and a
ground bearer, so each board spans 1.0 m.

Using the Haldane Fisher offer price instead of list for the eight 3.0 m
lengths saves about £28.

## Rope net

bearer top and the far side rail. It hangs from six M10 eye bolts in the rail

slack. Keep the mesh at 250–300 mm: the flexible-opening head-entrapment band

## A-frame end

rail for an A-frame: two raked legs from the same ground corners to under
the cross-beam ends, tied by an apex tie just under the beams, with a
trapezoid net laced into the A. This section is a delta from the cube list

### Hand-tied trapezoid net

The net fills the A between the bearer top (Z 244) and the apex tie
underside (Z 2061). A bought rectangular net no longer fits; a made-to-order
one (O'Rourke Playscapes, Huck) is the alternative.

- **Rows:** 6, every 300 mm at Z 244, 544, 844, 1144, 1444 and 1744, leg
  face to leg face: 1660, 1478, 1296, 1114, 933 and 751 long. The apex tie
  is the top rope.
- **Columns:** 4, at Y 550, 850, 1150 and 1450. The outer pair end on the
  legs at about Z 1500; the inner pair reach the tie.
- **Crossings:** 22, tied as for the cube net.
- **Rope:** about 39 m of 16 mm rope with hitching allowance; buy 60 m as two
  30 m blue polypropylene coils, £25.
- **Fixing:** horizontals hitch round each leg or through an eye bolt in its
  inner face; verticals tie off where they meet the leg. The V between a
  vertical and the leg points upward, which is safe, and the leg crosses the
  rows at 73 degrees, clear of the 60-degree V-opening limit.

### Checks (not an engineer's check)

- **Leg** as a 2252 mm column: about **4%** of its capacity (Euler buckling
  100 kN, crushing 71 kN, against about 3 kN).
- **Bearer:** **28%** at a 1 kN tie-down load.
- **Swing 2:** its seat edge at X = 2131 is 386 mm from the leg face and
  433 mm from the net plane.

### Net change

Against the cube-ended frame the A-frame end changes the project total by
**about −£45 to −£95**: the dropped rails and braces and the cheaper rope
outweigh the leg-top fixings.

## Appendix — beam checks (not an engineer's check)

This is a rough bending-stress estimate to choose section sizes. It is
**not an engineer's check**. It does not cover deflection, the bolted
connections, timber grade marking, moisture or the stiffness the knee
braces add. A competent person should confirm the design before anyone
builds it or uses it.

- **Load:** one adult of 100 kg on a swing, with a dynamic factor of 3 for
  swinging. That gives a 3 kN point load, applied conservatively at mid-span.
- **Section:** a 94 x 145 member on edge has a section modulus of
  94 x 145² / 6 = 329 400 mm³.
- **Cross beam:** the span is about 1.9 m, from the divider rail to the far
  side rail. M = 3 x 1.9 / 4 = 1.43 kNm, so the stress is 4.3 N/mm². That is
  **18%** of C24's 24 N/mm² characteristic bending strength.
- **Divider rail:** the span is about 1.8 m between the front and rear
  divider posts, with the beam reaction taken at mid-span. M = 3 x 1.8 / 4 =
  1.35 kNm, so the stress is 4.1 N/mm². That is **17%** of 24 N/mm².
- These percentages compare the stress with the characteristic strength.
  A design value, after the load-duration and material factors, is about
  two-thirds of 24 N/mm². Against that, the figures are roughly 26% and 25%,
  which still leaves a wide margin.
